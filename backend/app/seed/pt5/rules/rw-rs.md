# Rhetorical Synthesis
**Rule.** The goal is a checklist. The right answer meets EVERY part. Wrong answers are usually true facts from the notes that miss one part.
**Your trap.** You picked an accurate summary of her work that had no quotation, when the goal said "use a quotation". And you picked a choice that described a letter but never said its category, when the goal said "indicate which category".
**Method.**
1. Read the goal first. Split it into parts: a quotation + a claim, the study + its findings, both things + a difference.
2. Underline key nouns in the goal: "quotation", "category", "aims", "materials".
3. Check each choice against every part. One missing part = wrong.
4. Ignore how true or detailed a choice is.
**Watch for.**
- Quotation goals: look for quotation marks, then check the quote supports the claim.
- Category goals: the answer must say the category's name ("X is a commercial letter"), not only the facts or what X is not.
- "Unfamiliar audience": the answer must explain what the thing is.
