# Text Structure and Purpose
**Rule.** The function of an underlined part is the job it does for the sentences around it.
**Your trap.** The text described a character's habitual meekness and then showed him being cheated of land. You missed that the meek habit explains WHY he was cheated.
**Method.**
1. Read the sentence before and the sentence after the underlined part.
2. Name its job in one word: example, cause, contrast, limitation, background, result.
3. If the next sentence starts with "So" or shows an outcome, ask: does the underlined part explain why it happened?
4. For overall structure, label each sentence (assumption, finding, limitation ...) and pick the choice with the same labels in the same order.
**Watch for.**
- A habit ("he had never in his life ...") is not a later change.
- Check which outcome the underlined part explains, not just any outcome in the text.
- "However" after a finding often signals a limitation, not a new cause.
- One wrong step (often the last) makes a structure choice wrong.
