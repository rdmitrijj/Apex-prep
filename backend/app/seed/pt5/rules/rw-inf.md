# Inferences
**Rule.** The conclusion must match what the study actually varied or measured: not narrower, not broader, not the opposite.
**Your trap.** Two groups of spiders differed in one thing (reared in the field vs the lab). You picked a narrower claim about one detail instead of the conclusion about rearing conditions. You also missed why an unchanged variable mattered: it ruled out another explanation.
**Method.**
1. Name what differed between the groups. The conclusion is about that, as a whole.
2. If something "stayed the same", ask which other cause it rules out.
3. For an estimate, ask: does the method miss something (too low) or count extra (too high)?
4. To weaken a cause claim, find the effect without the cause.
**Watch for.**
- *Too narrow*: blames one part of a setting (field vs lab differ in many ways).
- *Goes beyond the text*: things the study never measured.
- *Reverses the direction*: overstates vs understates, strengthens vs weakens.
- Words like "only", "never", "proved" are usually too strong.
