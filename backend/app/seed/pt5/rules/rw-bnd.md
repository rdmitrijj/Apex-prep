# Boundaries
**Rule.** Extra information closes with the same mark that opened it (comma + comma, dash + dash). Two complete sentences need a period, semicolon, or colon, never a comma alone. Use a colon when the second sentence explains the first.
**Your trap.** You left out the closing comma after an appositive that followed a book title ("sisters" instead of "sisters,"). You also used a comma between two sentences where the second explained the first; use a colon.
**Method.**
1. Find the main subject and its verb. Cross out the extra words between them.
2. Close the extra words with the mark that opened them.
3. Between clauses, test each side: could it end with a period?
4. If both are complete and the second explains the first, use a colon.
**Watch for.**
- A long description after a title still needs a closing comma.
- Never mix marks: a comma can't close a dash.
- "However" and "instead" don't join sentences.
- No punctuation between a subject and its verb, or a verb and its object.
