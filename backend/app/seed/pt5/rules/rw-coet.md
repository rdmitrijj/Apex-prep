# Command of Evidence: Textual
**Rule.** The right quotation or finding proves the WHOLE claim, not just part of it.
**Your trap.** The claim said the poem was ambivalent (it shows merit AND displeasure). You picked a quotation that showed only one of the two feelings.
**Method.**
1. Before you read the choices, turn the claim into a checklist: "ambivalent" = two boxes (likes it / dislikes it); "changes" = two boxes (before / after).
2. Test every choice against every box. One missing box = wrong.
3. Check that both feelings are about the thing the claim names, and that a change goes in the right direction.
4. Support or weaken: does the finding show the cause and the effect moving together (support), or the effect happening without the cause (weaken)?
**Watch for.**
- A quotation with two feelings about the wrong thing ("wrong pair").
- A change in the opposite direction (pride to shame instead of shame to pride).
- A finding that has the right result but leaves another explanation open.
