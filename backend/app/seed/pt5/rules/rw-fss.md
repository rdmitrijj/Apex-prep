# Form, Structure, and Sense
**Rule.** An opening phrase ("Woven from...," "Having surveyed...," "Built in...,") describes the first noun after the comma. A verb agrees with its subject, not with the nearest noun.
**Your trap.** Dangling modifier, 2nd time this month: after "Woven from recycled yarn," you picked the artist, but the tapestries were woven. You also picked "underlies" for the plural "frameworks" because singular nouns sat in the clause and parentheses between them.
**Method.**
1. Opening phrase: ask "who or what did this?" before you read the choices.
2. Find the main noun right after the comma. In "the artist's tapestries" the main noun is "tapestries", not the artist.
3. Verb blank: cross out every relative clause ("that...", "which..."), every parenthesis, and every "of..." phrase.
4. Read subject + blank, then match singular or plural.
**Watch for.**
- A possessive person ("Halloran's book") does not fix a phrase about a person.
- The passive voice is correct when it puts the described object first.
- If the subject has no main verb yet, reject -ing, "to", and "having" forms.
