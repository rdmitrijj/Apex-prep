# Command of Evidence: Quantitative
**Rule.** The answer must use the data to prove the *exact* claim: the same groups, the same time, the same kind of comparison. Most wrong choices are true, but true is not enough.
**Your trap.** You picked a true fact that used the wrong groups or the wrong time (short vs long leave *before* the leave), or the most eye-catching number instead of the one that proves "not always".
**Method.**
1. Before reading the choices, underline the claim's two groups and its time ("after treatment").
2. Name the comparison: "X is not higher than Y", "the gap got smaller", or "at least one exception".
3. Find those numbers in the figure.
4. Cross out every choice with another group, time, or comparison.
**Watch for.**
- "Before" data uses the right groups but proves nothing about a benefit.
- The no-treatment control group is usually a distractor.
- "Not always" needs the exception, not the biggest number.
- "The gap narrowed" needs the gap at two times, not "always ahead".
